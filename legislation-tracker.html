How a bill becomes law
First reading
The bill is tabled and published. No debate.
Second reading
Debate on the bill's main idea, then a vote to send it to committee.
Committee
MPs or senators hear witnesses and go through the bill clause by clause. Most amendments happen here.
Report stage
The whole chamber considers the committee's changes and can amend further.
Third reading
Final debate and vote in that chamber. The bill then goes to the other chamber, which repeats these steps.
Royal assent
Once both chambers pass the same text, the Governor General's assent makes it law. Some or all of it may come into force later, by order or on a set date.
Awaiting a debate slot
Private members' bills are debated only once their sponsor's name is drawn for the Order of Precedence. Many never reach debate.
