House Publications

The Debates are the report—transcribed, edited, and corrected—of what is said in the House. The Journals are the official record of the decisions and other transactions of the House. The Order Paper and Notice Paper contains the listing of all items that may be brought forward on a particular sitting day, and notices for upcoming items.

For an advanced search, use Publication Search tool.

If you have any questions or comments regarding the accessibility of this publication, please contact us at accessible@parl.gc.ca.

Friday, June 12, 2026

10:00 a.m.

Introduction of Government Bills

Introduction of Private Members' Bills
June 11, 2026 — Corey Tochor (Saskatoon—University) — Bill entitled “An act to amend the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act and the Food and Drugs Act”.

Notices of Motions (Routine Proceedings)
June 11, 2026 — Rosemarie Falk (Battlefords—Lloydminster—Meadow Lake) — That the 10th report of the Standing Committee on Human Resources, Skills and Social Development and the Status of Persons with Disabilities, presented on Tuesday, May 26, 2026, be concurred in.
June 11, 2026 — Eric Melillo (Kenora—Kiiwetinoong) — That the fifth report of the Standing Committee on Indigenous and Northern Affairs, presented on Wednesday, June 10, 2026, be concurred in.
June 11, 2026 — Richard Martel (Chicoutimi—Le Fjord) — That the third report of the Standing Committee on Natural Resources, presented on Monday, May 25, 2026, be concurred in.
June 11, 2026 — Joël Godin (Portneuf—Jacques-Cartier) — That the fifth report of the Standing Committee on Official Languages, presented on Thursday, May 28, 2026, be concurred in.

Questions
Q-12872 — June 11, 2026 — Kelly McCauley (Edmonton West) — With regard to the details of certain contracts being withheld from disclosure, since January 1, 2022: (a) what was the total (i) number, (ii) value, of contracts which had their details withheld due a national security exemption, broken down by year; (b) what is the total (i) number, (ii) value, of contracts which had their details withheld for a reason other than national security, broken down by year and reason for the withholding of details; (c) what is the value of contracts in (a) and (b) broken down by (i) under $200,000, (ii) between $200,000 and $1,000,000, (iii) over $1,000,000; and (d) for each national security exemption signed, what was the (i) date, (ii) name of the official, (iii) title of the official, (iv) commodity, (v) specific national security rationale cited to support the exemption?
Q-12882 — June 11, 2026 — The Honourable Andrew Scheer (Regina—Qu'Appelle) — With regard to the usage of the government's fleet of aircraft, broken down by type of aircraft (Challenger, Polaris, Airbus, etc.) since February 1, 2026: what are the details of the legs of each flight, including the (i) date, (ii) point of departure, (iii) destination, (iv) number of passengers, (v) names and titles of the passengers, excluding security or Canadian Armed Forces members, (vi) total catering bill related to the flight, (vii) volume of fuel used, or an estimate, (viii) amount spent on fuel?
Q-12892 — June 11, 2026 — James Bezan (Selkirk—Interlake—Eastman) — With regard to the Canadian Armed Forces: (a) since December 5, 2022, how many members were recruited into the Canadian Armed Forces used fraudulent or otherwise invalid university degrees, broken down by, the (i) the Regular Force, (ii) the Reserve Force, (iii) Military Occupational Structure identification, (iv) rank; (b) since January 1, 2020, how many members graduated from the Regular Officer Training Plan with a degree who have subsequently failed the Development Period 1.1 (officially known as the Infantry Dismounted Platoon Commander Course), resulting in failing to meet their Employment Functional Point, broken down by (i) year, (ii) Military Occupational Structure identification; and (c) of the graduates from the Regular Officer Training Plan who failed to meet their Employment Functional Point, how many were released from the Canadian Armed Forces before completing their mandatory five year service obligation?
Q-12902 — June 11, 2026 — Xavier Barsalou-Duval (Pierre-Boucher—Les Patriotes—Verchères) — With regard to the program for reimbursing the expenses of governors general: what are the details of the reimbursed expenses, broken down by governor general and by year, based on all data currently available, including (i) a description of the expense, (ii) the date, (iii) the cost, (iv) any other available information?
Q-12912 — June 11, 2026 — Xavier Barsalou-Duval (Pierre-Boucher—Les Patriotes—Verchères) — With regard to the expenditures of the Office of the Secretary to the Governor General, the Department of National Defence, Global Affairs Canada, the Department of Canadian Heritage, the National Capital Commission and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police for the Governor General’s trips to Norway and Denmark (including Greenland), from February 2 to 7, 2026, as well as to Mexico from January 19 to 21, 2026, broken down by department and trip: what was the (i) cost of air and ground transportation, (ii) cost of meals during transport and at destination, including the list of meals, (iii) number of accompanying persons who made the trip and their role, (iv) cost of transportation and security staff and their number and role, (v) cost of accommodation and the list of locations, (vi) cost of travel arrangement fees, (vii) value of receipts submitted by the various staff and accompanying persons, (viii) amount of all other costs related to the trips?
Q-12922 — June 11, 2026 — Kelly Block (Carlton Trail—Eagle Creek) — With regard to focus groups or market research studies, conducted by government departments and agencies, broken down by each of the last two fiscal years: (a) how many were conducted; (b) how much was spent; (c) what are the details of all contracts for such services, including, for each, the (i) date, (ii) vendor, (iii) value, (iv) description of the goods or services provided; and (d) for each contract, focus group or study, what (i) are the details for each (ii) are the details of the research methods used (focus groups, surveys, etc.), (ii) are the details of the topics analyzed, (iii) are the details of the findings, (iv) is the title of the individual who oversaw the project?
Q-12932 — June 11, 2026 — Kelly Block (Carlton Trail—Eagle Creek) — With regard to the Treasury Board's Policy on transfer payments: (a) how many grant and contribution agreements have been issued in each of the last five years; (b) how many of the agreements in (a) were audited, in total and broken down by type of audit; (c) what is the breakdown of audits in (b), by reason (random selection, rotating audit, suspicion of non-compliance, etc.); (d) what is the breakdown of (b) and (c), by audit outcome, including, how many and what percentage of audits found non-compliance; and (e) for the agreements in non-compliance, what is the breakdown of consequences (repayment of funds, requirement to take corrective action to be in compliance, etc.)?
Q-12942 — June 11, 2026 — Ted Falk (Provencher) — With regard to the role of Health Canada, the Public Health Agency of Canada, and the government in the COVID-19 pandemic response: (a) what are the details of all analyses, briefing materials, evidence, or decision-making frameworks which supported the use of population-wide lockdown measures in the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic, given that section 4.3 of Canadian Pandemic Influenza Preparedness: Planning Guidance for the Health Sector states that such measures are "impractical, if not impossible"; (b) did the Public Health Agency of Canada conduct formal documented risk assessments at the outset of the COVID-19 pandemic, including those described in section 3.5.2 of Canadian Pandemic Influenza Preparedness addressing (i) viral characteristics, (ii) anticipated or observed impacts on the healthcare system and community, (iii) affected age and risk groups, (iv) antiviral resistance, (v) estimated effectiveness of control measures, including any interim, preliminary, or continuously updated assessments, and, if not, why not; (c) if the answer to (b) is affirmative, for each assessment, was are the details, including the (i) title or identifier, (ii) date initiated, (iii) date completed, (iv) details on whether the assessment was documented in written form, (v) summary of the findings; (d) were the assessments referred to in (c) made public, and, if so, for each, what was the (i) date of release, (ii) method of communication, (iii) summary of information disclosed, including links, publication titles, or specific public releases where available, and if not, why not; (e) what evidence, analyses, or guidance informed the federal government's recommendations regarding (i) mass testing and contact tracing, (ii) quarantine of exposed individuals, (iii) border closures, (iv) border entry and exit screening, in light of the World Health Organization publication titled Non-pharmaceutical public health measures for mitigating the risk and impact of epidemic and pandemic influenza, including any analyses or memoranda comparing Canadian measures to that guidance; (f) in the document titled Guidance for a strategic approach to lifting restrictive public health measures, how did the federal government define "significant population immunity", and what quantitative thresholds, models, indicators, or criteria were used to determine when restrictive public health measures would be lifted or maintained; (g) did the government conduct analyses to assess and quantify the potential impacts of restrictive public health measures, including health, economic, social, educational, and mental health impacts, including analyses conducted prior to or during implementation of such measures, and if so, for each analysis, (i) what is the date, (ii) what is the title, (iii) who is the responsible department, (iv) who is the approving authority, (v) what are the key findings, and if not, why not; (h) were the analyses referred to in (g) made public, and, if so, when and by what means, including links, publication titles, or specific public releases where available; if not, why not; (i) what categories of adverse impacts, including health, economic, and social effects, were considered in federal assessments of COVID-19 response measures, and how were these categories were incorporated into decision-making processes; (j) what federal programs or data sources were used to monitor (i) health outcomes, (ii) broader societal impacts associated with COVID-19 public health measures, including any reports, dashboards, or summaries produced from these data sources, and what were the key findings of those programs; (k) did officials within Health Canada, the Public Health Agency of Canada, central agencies, or the Prime Minister's Office review the July 2020 open letter titled Dealing with COVID-19 and, A Balanced Response, and if so, which officials reviewed it, how was it reviewed, and on what dates; (l) what consideration was given to the recommendations contained in the letter referenced in (k), including which recommendations, if any, were adopted or rejected, and the rationale for those decisions, including any written evaluations, summaries, or decision records assessing those recommendations; and (m) what are the details of all briefing notes, memoranda, or decision documents prepared for, or provided to, federal ministers, central agencies, or the Prime Minister's Office between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2021 regarding COVID-19 public health measures, including but not limited to materials prepared by Health Canada, the Public Health Agency of Canada, or other federal departments, and including for each (i) title or subject, (ii) date, (iii) originating department or agency?
Q-12952 — June 11, 2026 — Shuvaloy Majumdar (Calgary Heritage) — With regard to international assistance provided by Global Affairs Canada, since January 1, 2023: (a) does Global Affairs Canada maintain a centralized system that records implementing partners for all international assistance programs beyond the first-tier recipient, and, if so, what is the name of this system; and (b) if the answer to (a) is no, what systems are used instead to track implementing partners; (c) is the recording of implementing partners mandatory for all international assistance programs; (d) broken down by each year since 2023 were there any programs for which implementing partner information was not recorded, and, if so, how many and which ones; (e) for how many programs does Global Affairs Canada have complete records of all implementing partners involved in program delivery; (f) for how many programs are implementing partner records incomplete or partially unavailable; (g) for each funding channel (bilateral, multilateral, United Nations agencies), what percentage of programs include full implementing partner disclosure; (h) were intermediary organizations contractually required in all cases to disclose implementing partners as a condition of receiving funds, and, if not, what are the names of all programs where this requirement did not apply; (i) how many programs cannot be fully traced from initial Government of Canada disbursement to the level of final implementing partners; (j) for programs where implementing partners are not fully identifiable, what are their names and reasons for the lack of traceability; (k) for each instance in (j) what steps, if any, is Global Affairs Canada taking to address the situation; (l) does Global Affairs Canada take the position that the current system is sufficient to ensure full transparency of implementing partners for all international assistance funding, and, if so, on what basis is this assessment made; and (m) if the answer to (l) is no, what gaps have been identified in the system?
Q-12962 — June 11, 2026 — Kelly McCauley (Edmonton West) — With regard to government expenses related to the newly installed Governor General: (a) what are the projected costs associated with the transition, in total and broken down by type of expense (moving expenses, severance for staff departures, new furniture in Rideau Cottage, etc.); (b) what are the details of all expenditures on clothing, uniforms, ceremonial attire, or outerwear for the incoming Governor General, including, for each, the (i) date of purchase, (ii) amount spent and value of the item, (iii) description of the item; and (c) what was the budget and expected costs for the installation ceremony, banquet, and related events for the new Governor General held on June 8, in total and broken down by type of expense?
Q-12972 — June 11, 2026 — Mel Arnold (Kamloops—Shuswap—Central Rockies) — With regard to the government's Canada Strong Pass initiative that offers free admission and a 25% discount on fees for camping and overnight stays for all Parks Canada places from June 19 to September 7, 2026: (a) what are Parks Canada's projections as to the amount of revenue that will be lost due to the (i) 25% discount on camping and overnight stays, (ii) free admission, offered by this pass; and (b) what is Parks Canada's projection on the total amount of revenue that will be lost due to this pass?
Q-12982 — June 11, 2026 — Mel Arnold (Kamloops—Shuswap—Central Rockies) — With regard to the Department of Fisheries and Oceans' document titled "2026 Southern Resident Killer Whale Prey Measure Options Consultation Summary" distributed by the department on May 7, 2026: (a) how many respondents were there in the consultation; (b) how many First Nations supported maintaining or expanding the existing area-based closures and implementing additional Chinook restrictions; (c) how many First Nations called for expanded closures in additional key foraging areas; (d) how many First Nations emphasized the importance of stronger enforcement and monitoring; (e) which Indigenous rights and cultural responsibilities did First Nations stress are inseparable from Southern Resident Killer Whale survival; (f) how many groups supported expanded and earlier fishing closures in key foraging areas; and (g) how many groups strongly opposed additional restrictions?
Q-12992 — June 11, 2026 — Mel Arnold (Kamloops—Shuswap—Central Rockies) — With regard to the Department of Fisheries and Oceans' enforcement of the Fisheries Act and associated regulations governing fisheries, for each year since January 1, 2015: (a) how many Conservation and Protection personnel have been deployed for enforcement activities in each of Department of Fisheries and Oceans' seven operating regions; (b) how many persons have been charged in each region with offences as a result of Conservation and Protection enforcement activities; (c) how many charges have been laid in each region as a result of Conservation and Protection enforcement activities; (d) how many charges in each region were related to illegal, unreported or unregulated fishing; and (e) how many persons charged with offences have been convicted of charges in each region?
Q-13002 — June 11, 2026 — Mel Arnold (Kamloops—Shuswap—Central Rockies) — With regard to the Canadian Food Inspection Agency's regulation of commercial salmon farm aquaculture operations, for each year since January 1, 2015: (a) how many orders did the Canadian Food Inspection Agency's issue for the destruction of fish; (b) what was the location of each order; (c) what were the species and the number of fish the Canadian Food Inspection Agency ordered to be destroyed with each order; (d) what was the total amount of compensation paid to aquaculture operators for each order; and (e) what was the reason for each order?
