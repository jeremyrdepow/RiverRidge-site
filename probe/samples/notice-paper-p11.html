House Publications

The Debates are the report—transcribed, edited, and corrected—of what is said in the House. The Journals are the official record of the decisions and other transactions of the House. The Order Paper and Notice Paper contains the listing of all items that may be brought forward on a particular sitting day, and notices for upcoming items.

For an advanced search, use Publication Search tool.

If you have any questions or comments regarding the accessibility of this publication, please contact us at accessible@parl.gc.ca.

No. 156

Tuesday, October 27, 2026

10:00 a.m.

Introduction of Government Bills

Introduction of Private Members' Bills

Notices of Motions (Routine Proceedings)

Questions
Q-16412 — October 26, 2026 — Ellis Ross (Skeena—Bulkley Valley) — With regard to the government’s requirements for First Nations consent for proposed pipeline projects: (a) does the government require the consent of every First Nation potentially affected by a proposed project before the project may proceed; (b) is there a threshold level of support from impacted First Nations for the government to proceed with a proposed pipeline and, if so, what is it; (c) does the absence of unanimous support, even when overwhelming support is present, automatically prevent projects from proceeding; (d) what legislation, regulations, policies or guidelines govern the determination in (b) and (c); (e) how does the government distinguish between its duty to consult and accommodate First Nations and any requirement to obtain their consent; (f) how does the government weigh the interests of First Nations that support a project, including those seeking equity ownership, employment or revenue, against those of First Nations that oppose it; and (g) what process exists to resolve disagreements among potentially affected First Nations, and who has the authority to make the final decision on whether the project may proceed?
Q-16422 — October 26, 2026 — Ellis Ross (Skeena—Bulkley Valley) — With regard to the government's efforts to address textile and apparel waste and microfibre pollution in Canada: (a) for each year since 2015, to the most recent year for which data is available, what was the (i) total quantity of textile and apparel waste discarded and not recycled or reused, (ii) total quantity of textile and apparel waste recycled, (iii) total quantity of textile and apparel waste reused; (b) what are the details of all federal funding provided since January 1, 2020, for projects related to textile and apparel waste reduction, recycling, reuse, circular economy initiatives and textile-related pollution reduction, including, for each project, the (i) recipient, (ii) funding program, (iii) date, (iv) amount approved, (v) amount disbursed, (vi) type of funding, (vii) project description, (viii) project location; (c) what are the details of all government action taken since July 1, 2024, in relation to the development of the roadmap for addressing plastic waste and pollution from the textile and apparel sector, including the date of each action; (d) what is the current status of the roadmap referred to in (c); (e) has a draft or final version of the roadmap been completed and, if so, what are the details, including (i) what version was completed, (ii) on what date, (iii) which departments or agencies created the roadmap, (iv) what is the title, (v) when will the document be publicly released; (f) what steps, if any, have been taken to include textiles and apparel in the Federal Plastics Registry; (g) what textile and apparel products or categories are being considered for inclusion in the Federal Plastics Registry; (h) what are the planned implementation dates for Phase 2 and Phase 3 of the Federal Plastics Registry; and (i) what are the details of any documents produced since January 1, 2024, regarding the inclusion of textiles and apparel in the Federal Plastics Registry, including, for each, the (i) date, (ii) sender, (iii) recipient, (iv) type of document, (v) title, (vi) summary of contents?
Q-16432 — October 26, 2026 — The Honourable Andrew Scheer (Regina—Qu'Appelle) — With regard to the government's advertising programs or campaigns funded since January 1, 2025, including those which are still ongoing: (a) what are the total amounts allocated and spent on government advertising programs through (i) the Privy Council Office, (ii) Public Services and Procurement Canada, (iii) Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, (iv) Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada during this period; (b) what are the details of each advertising campaign related to the amounts in (a), including the (i) amount spent, (ii) dates the campaign ran, (iii) purpose or theme of the campaign, (iv) medium or platforms used; and (c) what are the details of each contract related to the campaigns in (a), including, for each, (i) the vendor, (ii) the date, (iii) the amount, (iv) the description of the goods or services, (v) whether the contract was sole-sourced or competitive bid?
Q-16442 — October 26, 2026 — William Stevenson (Yellowhead) — With regard to bonuses awarded at Parks Canada and the Canada Infrastructure Bank for the 2025-26 fiscal year and broken down by each: (a) what was the total amount awarded in bonuses; (b) how many and what percentage of officials (i) at or above the executive level or equivalent, (ii) below the executive level or equivalent, received bonuses; and (c) of the amount awarded in bonuses, how much went to officials (i) at or above the executive level or equivalent, (ii) below the executive level or equivalent?
Q-16452 — October 26, 2026 — David McKenzie (Calgary Signal Hill) — With regard to immigration data and Statistics Canada's September 2026 revised non-permanent resident population estimates, which report an annual net increase of 703,206 between July 1, 2023, and July 1, 2024, compared with only 34,305 between July 1, 2024, and July 1, 2025, representing a 95.1% reduction in annual net population growth: (a) for each of the periods from July 1, 2023, to June 30, 2024, and from July 1, 2024, to June 30, 2025, what are the numerical values for (i) the non-permanent resident population at the beginning and end of each period, (ii) the number of new non-permanent residents entering Canada, (iii) the number of non-permanent residents physically departing Canada, separately identifying departures verified through Canada Border Services Agency records and departures estimated or presumed, (iv) the number transitioning to permanent resident status, (v) the number changing between work permit, study permit, asylum-related and other non-permanent resident categories, (vi) the number removed from non-permanent resident population estimates solely because their permits expired without a recorded extension or qualifying status, (vii) the number identified in (vi) for whom no physical departure from Canada was verified, (viii) all other statistical or administrative adjustments affecting the non-permanent resident population estimate, (ix) a complete numerical reconciliation demonstrating how the foregoing components produced the reported annual net increases of 703,206 and 34,305, respectively; (b) for each component, what data, estimates or assumptions is the figure based on; (c) for each year from 2022 through the latest available date in 2026 (i) how many unique individuals held work permits or study permits that expired, (ii) how many received extensions or replacement permits, (iii) how many had maintained status while awaiting a decision, (iv) how many transitioned to permanent residence, (v) how many were removed from non-permanent resident estimates following permit expiration, (vi) how many of those removals were supported by confirmed Canada Border Services Agency departure records, (vii) how many were not supported by a verified departure; (d) can the Canada Border Services Agency match temporary resident permit expirations with individual physical departure records, and, if so, (i) how many temporary residents whose permits expired had a verified departure, (ii) how many had neither a verified departure nor a subsequent recorded immigration authorization, (iii) what proportion of presumed departures in Statistics Canada's non-permanent resident estimates is independently verified, (iv) what statistical adjustments account for people who remain in Canada after their permits expire; (e) with regard to the September 2026 statistical revisions (i) how many additional non-permanent residents were identified through previously unavailable permit-extension information, (ii) what numerical changes resulted from incorporating additional Canada Border Services Agency entry-exit information, (iii) what was the impact of each methodological revision on annual non-permanent resident estimates for 2022 through 2026, (iv) how many individuals previously excluded from non-permanent resident estimates were subsequently restored because of additional immigration-status records, (v) what steps have been taken to prevent similar errors in future estimates; (f) what margins of error, uncertainty estimates or sensitivity analyses have been calculated for the non-permanent resident population estimates; (g) has Statistics Canada estimated the potential undercount arising from individuals who remain in Canada following permit expiration; (h) what evidence supports the reported 95.1% reduction in annual net non-permanent resident growth; (i) what proportion of the reduction is attributable to changes in arrivals, verified departures, permanent residence transitions, permit expirations and statistical adjustments; (j) what independent verification has been conducted to assess the accuracy of the revised non-permanent resident estimates; and (k) will the government publish an annual reconciliation distinguishing verified departures, presumed departures, permit expirations and permanent residence transitions, and, if so, when?
Q-16462 — October 26, 2026 — David McKenzie (Calgary Signal Hill) — With regard to the Fabricated Industrial Steel Components Anti-dumping and Countervailing Duty Remission Order (SOR/2019-297), authorized by Order in Council P.C. 2019-1189, which provides remission of certain anti-dumping and countervailing duties on fabricated industrial steel components imported for the LNG Canada project in Kitimat, British Columbia, and the Woodfibre LNG project in Squamish, British Columbia: (a) broken down by calendar year and by each LNG project, since August 8, 2019, what are the (i) total quantities of fabricated industrial steel components imported under the remission order, expressed in metric tonnes, (ii) total declared customs value of those imports, expressed in Canadian dollars, (iii) country of origin and country of manufacture of the imported steel components, (iv) number of customs declarations submitted claiming remission under the order, (v) total value of imported fabricated industrial steel components originating in China; (b) for each calendar year since 2019 and for each project, what are the (i) total anti-dumping duties remitted, waived or refunded, (ii) total countervailing duties remitted, waived or refunded, (iii) combined value of all duties remitted under the order, (iv) amount of duty relief attributable specifically to steel originating in China, (v) applicable anti-dumping and countervailing duty rates that would otherwise have applied to those imports; (c) what are the details of the Canada Border Services Agency's administration of the remission order, including (i) what customs accounting codes, special authority identifiers or other administrative references are used to identify import transactions claiming relief under SOR/2019-297, (ii) whether the Canada Border Services Agency maintains transaction-level records identifying the importer, date of importation, customs value, quantity, country of origin and duties remitted, (iii) whether the Canada Border Services Agency has kept track of all import transactions associated with this order since its implementation, (iv) what quantity measurements are recorded; (d) how many compliance reviews or audits have been conducted to verify that imported components qualified for remission, and what quantities and values of imports were determined to be ineligible; (e) what procedures ensure that imported components receiving remission were incorporated into the projects specified in the order; (f) has the government conducted any review of the continued necessity of the SOR/2019-297 remission order since 2019, and is the government planning on amending, restricting or repealing the order following the commencement of production at LNG Canada's first phase; (g) what quantities and values of qualifying steel components have been imported since July 1, 2025; (h) what amounts of anti-dumping and countervailing duties have been remitted since July 1, 2025; and (i) does the government anticipate additional imports qualifying for remission under the order, and, if so, what quantities and values are projected?
Q-16472 — October 26, 2026 — Dan Albas (Okanagan Lake West—South Kelowna) — With regard to the Air Passenger Protection Regulations, the complaints backlog at the Canadian Transportation Agency and the amendments to the Canada Transportation Act made by the Budget Implementation Act, 2023, No. 1 (S.C. 2023, c. 26) that are not yet in force, as of the date of this question: (a) how many air passenger complaints are currently awaiting a decision, in total and broken down by the length of time since the complaint was received (less than six months, six to 12 months, one to two years, more than two years); (b) what are the average and longest times to resolve a complaint in each of the last three years; (c) what are the projected costs associated with the proposal to transfer complaint handling to a private contractor, in total and broken down by type of cost; (d) what procurement process will be used for the transfer; (e) by what date is the backlog of complaints projected to be cleared; (f) on what date does the government expect the amended regulations, published in draft in the Canada Gazette, Part I, on December 21, 2024, to be finalized and come into force (for example what are the reasons the regulations have not yet been finalized); (g) how much has the Canadian Transportation Agency spent on complaint handling in each of the last three fiscal years, in total and broken down by type of expenditure (salaries, information technology, etc.); and (h) what are the details of any directive or communication the Minister of Transport has provided to the Canadian Transportation Agency since 2019 regarding air passenger complaints or the backlog, including, for each, the (i) date, (ii) recipients, (iii) type of communication, (iv) title, (v) summary of contents, including any instructions or direction provided, (vi) impact of the instruction or direction?
Q-16482 — October 26, 2026 — Arpan Khanna (Oxford) — With regard to the government's advertising programs or campaigns funded since January 1, 2025, including those which are still ongoing: (a) what are the total amounts allocated and spent on government advertising programs through Public Safety Canada or for any campaign related to the topics of crime, justice or public safety; (b) what are the details of each advertising campaign related to the amounts in (a), including the (i) amount spent, (ii) dates the campaign ran, (iii) purpose or theme of the campaign, (iv) medium or platforms used; and (c) what are the details of each contract related to the campaigns in (a), including, for each, (i) the vendor, (ii) the date, (iii) the amount, (iv) the description of the goods or services, (v) whether the contract was sole-sourced or competitive bid?
Q-16492 — October 26, 2026 — Vincent Ho (Richmond Hill South) — With regard to government information about the impact of the European Union's move to narrow Ukraine refugee protection and exclude men of military age, the Canada-Ukraine Authorization for Emergency Travel program and Ukrainian nationals residing in Canada: (a) how many Ukrainian nationals currently reside in Canada, in total and broken down by province or territory, immigration status, sex and age; (b) how many Ukrainian males of military age reside in Canada, in total and broken down by age, immigration status, province or territory, arrival year and whether or not they participated in the Canada-Ukraine Authorization for Emergency Travel program; (c) what are the details of any information or requests concerning these military obligations that Canada has exchanged with the government of Ukraine and the European Union, including, for each, the (i) date, (ii) sender, (iii) recipient, (iv) title, (v) summary of contents; (d) how many Ukrainian nationals' temporary authorizations have expired or are scheduled to expire each year from 2026 through 2030; (e) what are the details of any temporary authorization extensions planned by the government, including the eligibility and duration details; and (f) what policies and operational plans determine how the conditions in Ukraine affect the government's approach to Ukrainians whose visa or legal status in Canada has expired?
Q-16502 — October 26, 2026 — Michael Barrett (Leeds—Grenville—Thousand Islands—Rideau Lakes) — With regard to reports that some Veterans Affairs Canada employees are unnecessarily requiring veterans to withdraw and resubmit applications to avoid processing times that exceed service standards from negatively impacting the department's performance reports: (a) broken down by year since 2021 and by type of application, how many instances have occurred where employees have required or requested veterans to withdraw and resubmit their applications, in total, and broken down by the reasons for withdrawal and resubmission; (b) broken down by year since 2021 and by type of application, how many instances have occurred where veterans have withdrawn and then later resubmitted their applications; (c) broken down by year since 2021 and by each service standard target, what are the departmental performance results when taking the withdrawn and resubmitted applications into account for the calculation; and (d) what are the details of any directives provided by the Minister or executives at Veterans Affairs Canada from discouraging these unnecessary application withdrawals?
Q-16512 — October 26, 2026 — Tony Baldinelli (Niagara Falls—Niagara-on-the-Lake) — With regard to the government's purchases of clothing and apparel manufactured outside Canada, including purchases for resale (such as at gift shops operated by Parks Canada), broken down by department or agency: (a) what are the details of all purchases of apparel from manufacturers outside of Canada, since January 1, 2025, including, for each, the (i) date, (ii) vendor, (iii) country of vendor, (iv) country apparel was manufactured, (v) contract value, (vi) description of goods, including volume, (vii) purpose for which the apparel was used (uniforms, resale, promotional items, etc.), (viii) reason why a Canadian manufacturer was not chosen; and (b) what is each department or agency's policy on when it is permitted to purchase foreign clothing and apparel and when to choose Canadian suppliers?
